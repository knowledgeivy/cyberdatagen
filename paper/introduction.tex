\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Generative AI technologies have enhanced productivity across numerous domains, accelerating knowledge dissemination and democratizing access to sophisticated content creation capabilities~\cite{baldassarre2023social}. However, this technological advancement has introduced profound challenges to cybersecurity, fundamentally altering both offensive and defensive capabilities~\cite{wach2023dark}. The dual-use nature of these technologies presents a critical question for security practitioners: do generative models ultimately favor attackers or defenders?

This question carries particular urgency in the domain of spam and phishing detection. Adversaries increasingly exploit LLMs to generate deceptive content at unprecedented scale~\cite{bethany2024large,weinz2025impact}. Unlike template-based generation methods that dominated previous eras, LLM-generated spam exhibits natural linguistic characteristics, grammatical correctness, and contextual coherence that closely approximate legitimate communications. The elimination of traditional indicators such as spelling errors, grammatical mistakes, and repetitive patterns fundamentally undermines detection heuristics developed over decades of adversarial evolution~\cite{magdy2022efficient}. Furthermore, LLMs enable sophisticated personalization strategies through integration with information gathering capabilities~\cite{yu2025survey,kim2025llms}, allowing adversaries to craft targeted spear phishing campaigns informed by publicly available intelligence about potential victims. This convergence of generation quality, operational scale, and personalization capacity represents a qualitative shift in the threat landscape.

Defenders confronting this evolved threat face their own fundamental challenges. Spam detection systems require large, diverse training datasets, yet acquisition of such data encounters critical obstacles including privacy regulations, legal constraints, and the inherent rarity of malicious samples in operational environments~\cite{li2024spamdam,bazzaz2021hybrid}. Traditional machine learning approaches to spam classification have demonstrated effectiveness across multiple paradigms~\cite{wang2025hybrid}, but all depend critically on training data that reflects the distributional characteristics of deployment environments. The same LLM technologies empowering attackers also present defenders with potential solutions to data scarcity through synthetic generation~\cite{zhang2025llms}. If synthetic spam can effectively substitute for real training data without degrading detection performance, defenders gain transformative capabilities for building detection systems without accessing sensitive data and rapidly adapting to emerging threats.

The central challenge, however, extends beyond training data augmentation. Organizations must defend against adversaries who may employ any of numerous capable language models for attack generation, yet defenders typically cannot predict which specific models adversaries will utilize. This asymmetry raises critical questions about cross-model generalization: can detection systems trained on synthetic data from accessible LLMs effectively recognize attacks generated by different LLM sources? If cross-model augmentation proves effective, defenders could proactively strengthen their systems by generating synthetic training data from available models, effectively leveraging generative AI to counter generative AI-powered attacks.

These considerations motivate three complementary research questions that systematically examine whether Generative AI favors attackers or defenders in spam detection:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{RQ1: Can LLM-generated synthetic spam effectively substitute for real training data?}
    \item \textbf{RQ2a: Can traditional spam detectors recognize LLM-generated attacks without prior exposure?}
    \item \textbf{RQ2b: Does augmenting training data with synthetic spam from available LLMs improve detection of attacks from different LLM sources?}
\end{enumerate}

This work makes several fundamental contributions to understanding Generative AI's role in spam detection:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Comprehensive Dual-Track Evaluation.} We provide the first systematic evaluation of LLMs' dual impact on spam detection, simultaneously assessing offensive capabilities for evasive spam generation and defensive capabilities for training data augmentation. This comprehensive approach reveals asymmetric advantages favoring attackers through accessible, high-quality generation capabilities, though defenders obtain measurable countermeasures through architectural choices and cross-model augmentation.

    \item \textbf{Rigorous Statistical Framework.} Through replicated experimental design across multiple models, prompts, and classifiers, we establish evaluation standards for synthetic cybersecurity data research that enable hypothesis testing with quantified uncertainty and effect magnitudes. Linear degradation models relating synthetic proportion to performance enable practitioners to predict detection effectiveness for arbitrary mixing ratios and establish operational deployment boundaries.

    \item \textbf{Cross-Model Generalization Analysis.} Our cross-LLM evaluation demonstrates that training with synthetic spam from one model significantly improves detection of attacks from different models, validating "fighting fire with fire" strategies. We establish quantitative performance patterns revealing substantial quality gaps between generation sources, demonstrating that model selection represents the most impactful decision for both offensive generation and defensive augmentation.

    \item \textbf{Architectural Robustness Analysis.} We demonstrate that classifier architecture fundamentally determines synthetic data utility: ensemble methods maintain stable performance regardless of synthetic proportion, while margin-based classifiers exhibit substantial sensitivity. This finding establishes architectural selection as a critical defensive capability, with ensemble methods providing inherent resilience against distributional shifts without requiring augmentation.

    \item \textbf{Evidence-Based Resolution of Offense-Defense Balance.} We provide empirically-grounded answers to whether Generative AI favors attackers or defenders, revealing fundamental asymmetries in capabilities, costs, and operational constraints. The precision-recall trade-offs we identify translate directly to operational guidance for both offensive evaluation and defensive deployment strategies.

    \item \textbf{Public Benchmark Dataset.} We release a comprehensive benchmark comprising LLM-generated spam samples across multiple models and prompt strategies, enabling reproducibility and standardized evaluation for future research.\footnote{Dataset is available at: \\url{https://github.com/tisage/llm-synthetic-spam-benchmark}}

\end{enumerate}
