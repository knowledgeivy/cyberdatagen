\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

Our evaluation systematically assesses LLM-generated synthetic spam across three complementary experimental tracks spanning 4,080 training-evaluation units. Following the methodology framework, we present results organized by research questions: Track 1 evaluates synthetic data for classifier training (RQ1), Track 2a establishes baseline detection capabilities against AI-powered spam (RQ2a), and Track 2b investigates hybrid training strategies (RQ2b).

As Figure~\ref{fig:track1} and Table~\ref{tab:track1} show, performance degradation follows highly linear patterns with increasing synthetic proportion, yielding $R^2 > 0.95$ for SVM configurations (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:statists}). Random Forest exhibits near-zero slopes across most configurations, indicating stable performance regardless of synthetic proportion.

Classifier architecture determines robustness to synthetic training data. Random Forest demonstrates substantially higher resilience than SVM across both generation models. For GPT at 100\% synthetic training, RF maintains near-baseline F1 (0.788-0.827 vs baseline 0.793), while SVM degrades significantly (0.617-0.735 vs baseline 0.849). For Claude original prompt, RF achieves F1 = 0.796 compared to SVM's 0.457. This architectural difference manifests in precision-recall trade-offs: SVM maintains near-perfect precision (0.95-1.00) while recall degrades substantially, resulting in overly conservative classification. RF maintains balanced profiles with both metrics stable under synthetic substitution, suggesting its ensemble voting mechanism provides more robust generalization than SVM's strict decision boundaries.

Prompt strategy affects effectiveness differently across classifiers. For SVM, original prompts consistently outperform alternatives across both models, establishing that faithful reproduction produces synthetic spam closest to real spam's feature distribution. RF exhibits non-monotonic behavior with prompt sensitivity varying by model.

GPT generates higher-quality synthetic training data than Claude across all configurations. At 100\% synthetic with original prompts, GPT maintains 86.6\% of baseline performance while Claude retains only 53.8\%. This quality gap persists across all prompt strategies and classifiers, indicating fundamental differences in generation capabilities.

As Figure~\ref{fig:track2a} and Table~\ref{tab:track2a_2b} show, classifier architecture determines sensitivity to training data quantity for zero-shot detection. SVM exhibits substantial monotonic improvement with additional real spam (GPT detection: F1 from 0.744 at 100 samples to 0.881 at 200 samples), while Random Forest maintains stable performance across training sizes (F1 $\approx$ 0.83 for GPT, $\approx$ 0.80 for Claude). This reflects architectural differences between margin-based and ensemble learning.

Detection difficulty varies by model and prompt strategy. GPT synthetic spam exhibits higher baseline detectability (SVM: 0.636-0.744, RF: 0.830-0.831) than Claude synthetic spam (SVM: 0.335-0.589, RF: 0.747-0.811) at 100 training samples. This higher detectability paradoxically correlates with GPT's superior performance in Track 1, suggesting distributional proximity to real spam enables both effective classifier training and detection. Weak prompts universally yield lowest detection (Claude weak: SVM F1 = 0.335), indicating prompt optimization for evasiveness differs by LLM source.

\subsection{Track 2b: Mixed-to-Synthetic Detection}
\label{sec:eval:track2b}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{pic/track2b_combined_gpt_claude.png}
\caption{Track 2b - Mixed-to-Synthetic Detection: Performance metrics (Precision, Recall, F1-Score) across prompt strategies (original/strong/weak, rows) and detection targets (columns). (a) Detecting GPT (trained on Claude synthetic): SVM peaks at 50\% synthetic (F1 = 0.77, +3.5\%) before declining at 100\% (F1 = 0.65, -13\%). RF improves substantially with cross-model augmentation (F1: 0.82 $\rightarrow$ 0.89, +8.5\%). (b) Detecting Claude (trained on GPT synthetic): SVM shows monotonic improvement (F1: 0.53 $\rightarrow$ 0.69, +30\% relative). RF maintains stable high performance (F1 $\approx$ 0.80) across all ratios.}
\label{fig:track2b}
\end{figure*}


As Figure~\ref{fig:track2b} and Table~\ref{tab:track2a_2b} show, training with synthetic spam from one LLM improves detection of another LLM's synthetic spam. For Claude detection using GPT synthetic training data, SVM achieves substantial improvements at 100\% synthetic (strong prompt: F1 from 0.590 to 0.860). For GPT detection using Claude synthetic training data, RF achieves F1 improvement from 0.824 to 0.894 for original prompt. Cross-model augmentation provides measurable benefits across configurations.

Augmentation effectiveness depends on prompt diversity rather than quality. For Claude detection, weak prompt training data achieves F1 = 0.805 compared to original prompt training (F1 = 0.688), suggesting diverse, lower-quality synthetic spam better captures the feature space of AI-generated content than high-quality samples alone.

Random Forest demonstrates superior cross-model robustness compared to SVM. For both GPT and Claude detection, RF maintains stable baseline performance across prompts (F1 $\approx$ 0.75-0.83) and achieves consistent improvements with synthetic augmentation. SVM exhibits substantial baseline variance (F1 range: 0.335-0.744) and less consistent augmentation benefits, indicating ensemble methods extract cross-model invariant features more effectively.

Optimal synthetic ratios vary by target model and classifier. For Claude detection using GPT synthetic training data, most configurations show monotonic improvement. For GPT detection using Claude synthetic training data, RF benefits from high synthetic ratios (optimal at 100\%, F1 = 0.834-0.908), while SVM demonstrates non-monotonic behavior with peak performance at 50\% before declining at 100\%. This suggests SVM overfits to distributional artifacts when trained exclusively on cross-model synthetic spam.
