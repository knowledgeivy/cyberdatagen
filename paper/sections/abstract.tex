\label{sec:abstract}
Large Language Models fundamentally reshape spam detection by enabling both synthetic training data generation and AI-powered spam attacks. We systematically evaluate this dual impact through three experimental tracks across two LLMs (GPT-4.1-mini and Claude-3.5-haiku), three prompt strategies, and two classifier architectures with 20 independent replications. Our experiments reveal three critical findings. First, LLM-generated synthetic spam can partially substitute for real training data with dramatic model-dependent quality gaps: GPT maintains 86\% of baseline performance at full synthetic training while Claude retains only 54\%. Second, zero-shot detection of AI-generated spam achieves moderate baseline performance, with substantial variation by target model and prompt strategy. Third, cross-model synthetic augmentation significantly improves detection effectiveness, with diversity-driven training providing larger gains than quality-optimized data. Random Forest consistently outperforms SVM through superior robustness to distributional shifts and cross-model generalization. These findings reveal asymmetric advantages favoring attackers through accessible generation capabilities, though defenders can leverage ensemble architectures and cross-model augmentation to maintain detection effectiveness. Our work provides empirical foundations for understanding Generative AI's role in cybersecurity offense-defense dynamics.
