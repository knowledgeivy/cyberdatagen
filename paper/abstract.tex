\begin{abstract}
Machine learning systems for cybersecurity face persistent challenges from data scarcity, privacy constraints, and class imbalance that limit the development of robust detection models. While Large Language Models (LLMs) offer promising approaches for synthetic cybersecurity data generation, current methods rely on ad-hoc prompt engineering without systematic evaluation of generation strategy effectiveness or comprehensive quality assessment.

This paper presents a systematic framework for evaluating LLM-generated synthetic cybersecurity data across multiple dimensions of quality and utility. We introduce a four-stage evaluation methodology that progresses from stratified seed selection through controlled synthetic generation to comprehensive performance assessment. Our framework enables rigorous comparison of prompt engineering strategies while accounting for the unique operational constraints of cybersecurity applications.

Through comprehensive experimentation across 16 dataset configurations and three model architectures, we evaluate three distinct prompt strategies: original (balanced preservation), strong (aggressive amplification), and weak (conservative subtlety). Our evaluation employs both embedding space analysis for intrinsic quality assessment and classifier performance measurement under realistic class imbalance conditions ranging from 5\% to 20\% malicious ratios.

Results demonstrate that synthetic data can achieve F1-scores within 0.012 of real data performance under optimal conditions, with the original prompt strategy consistently outperforming both strong and weak alternatives across all experimental configurations. Embedding space analysis reveals strong correlations between distributional similarity and classification performance, with synthetic data showing 73\% co-clustering rates with seed samples under optimal generation strategies. Notably, synthetic data maintains 93.4\% of real data performance under severe class imbalance conditions (5\% malicious ratio), demonstrating particular value for resource-constrained cybersecurity environments.

Our findings establish evidence-based guidance for cybersecurity prompt engineering while revealing that balanced approaches consistently outperform extreme strategies. The systematic framework provides standardized evaluation protocols that enable meaningful comparison across different synthetic generation approaches and establishes benchmarks for future research in synthetic cybersecurity data generation.
\end{abstract}
