\begin{abstract}

Machine learning-based spam detection faces persistent challenges from data scarcity, privacy constraints, and class imbalance, limiting development of robust classifiers. Large Language Models (LLMs) offer promise for generating synthetic training data, yet fundamental questions remain about their effectiveness across different classifier architectures, generation strategies, and operational conditions. We present a rigorous evaluation framework employing systematic replication ($R = 20$ independent groups) and comprehensive statistical analysis to assess LLM-generated synthetic spam data utility. Through 1,320 experimental configurations spanning three prompt strategies (original, strong, weak), two mixing approaches (within-group, cross-group), eleven synthetic proportions (0\%-100\%), and two classifier architectures (SVM, Random Forest), we establish quantitative relationships between synthetic data characteristics and classification performance. Our findings reveal striking architectural dependence: Random Forest demonstrates 11.8-fold greater robustness than SVM (2.1\% vs. 24.1\% degradation at 100\% synthetic), with fundamentally different degradation patterns (SVM: $R^2 = 0.990$ linear, Random Forest: $R^2 = 0.723$ non-linear). Contrary to expectations, prompt engineering intensity shows negligible impact (effect sizes $d < 0.1$), while distribution matching between synthetic and real samples proves less critical than anticipated (within-group vs. cross-group near-equivalence). The rigorous statistical framework—incorporating hypothesis testing with False Discovery Rate correction, Cohen's $d$ effect sizes, confidence intervals, and sensitivity analysis—transforms synthetic data evaluation from point estimation to confirmatory hypothesis testing with quantified uncertainty. These findings provide evidence-based guidance for operational deployment: prioritize ensemble classifiers for synthetic data scenarios, focus optimization on model selection rather than prompt tuning, and leverage distribution matching flexibility for simplified deployment. The systematic methodology establishes evaluation standards for synthetic cybersecurity data research while enabling practitioners to predict performance impacts and make informed decisions about synthetic data integration in operational spam detection systems.

\end{abstract}
