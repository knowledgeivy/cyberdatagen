\begin{abstract}

Large Language Models fundamentally reshape spam detection by enabling both synthetic training data generation and AI-powered spam attacks. We systematically evaluate this dual impact through 2,280 experiments spanning two LLMs (GPT-4.1-mini, Claude-3.5-Haiku), three prompt strategies, multiple mixing ratios, and two classifier architectures across 20 independent replications. Our Synthetic-to-Real Detection experiments reveal model-dependent degradation: GPT maintains 73-87\% baseline F1 at 100\% synthetic training while Claude retains only 46-54\%, establishing a 2-4$\times$ quality gap. Synthetic-trained classifiers exhibit precision-recall asymmetry (precision: 0.95-1.00, recall: 0.30-0.60), capturing surface features but missing distributional diversity. Real-to-Synthetic Detection shows moderate zero-shot AI-spam detection (F1: 0.532-0.824), with original prompts producing the most evasive spam. Mixed-to-Synthetic Detection demonstrates cross-model augmentation improves detection by 29-46\% (Claude) or 8-10\% (GPT), though diversity outweighs quality for cross-model generalization. Random Forest consistently outperforms SVM across all tracks (variance $<$5\% vs 40-76\%). These findings reveal asymmetric advantages: attackers gain accessible, high-quality generation tools (\$1.80 for 6,000 emails, 73\% training effectiveness) while defenders require architectural choices, cross-model augmentation, and acceptance of precision-recall trade-offs. Generative AI favors attackers unless defenders proactively adopt ensemble architectures and cross-model strategies.

\end{abstract}
