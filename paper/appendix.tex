\subsection{Performance Metrics: Mathematical Definitions}
\label{appendix:metrics}

We employ a comprehensive suite of classification performance metrics to evaluate synthetic data effectiveness. All metrics are computed on the fixed out-of-sample test set, where $TP$ (True Positives), $TN$ (True Negatives), $FP$ (False Positives), and $FN$ (False Negatives) represent counts from the confusion matrix.

\subsubsection{Primary Classification Metrics}

\paragraph{Accuracy} Overall classification correctness across both classes:
\begin{equation}
\text{Accuracy} = \frac{TP + TN}{TP + TN + FP + FN}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Precision} Proportion of predicted spam that is truly spam, measuring false positive control:
\begin{equation}
\text{Precision} = \frac{TP}{TP + FP}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Recall} Proportion of actual spam correctly identified, measuring detection sensitivity:
\begin{equation}
\text{Recall} = \frac{TP}{TP + FN}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{F1-Score} Harmonic mean of precision and recall, providing balanced assessment under class imbalance:
\begin{equation}
\text{F1} = 2 \cdot \frac{\text{Precision} \cdot \text{Recall}}{\text{Precision} + \text{Recall}} = \frac{2 \cdot TP}{2 \cdot TP + FP + FN}
\end{equation}

The F1-score serves as our primary performance metric due to its balanced treatment of precision and recall, particularly important given the 10\% spam class imbalance in our experimental design.

\paragraph{Specificity} True negative rate measuring correct identification of legitimate emails:
\begin{equation}
\text{Specificity} = \frac{TN}{TN + FP}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC)} Balanced measure considering all confusion matrix elements, robust to class imbalance:
\begin{equation}
\text{MCC} = \frac{TP \cdot TN - FP \cdot FN}{\sqrt{(TP + FP)(TP + FN)(TN + FP)(TN + FN)}}
\end{equation}

MCC ranges from -1 (total disagreement) to +1 (perfect prediction), with 0 indicating random performance.

\subsubsection{Threshold-Independent Metrics}

\paragraph{AUC-ROC} Area under the Receiver Operating Characteristic curve, measuring discrimination ability across all classification thresholds. The ROC curve plots True Positive Rate (TPR = Recall) against False Positive Rate (FPR = 1 - Specificity) at varying decision thresholds. AUC-ROC = 1.0 indicates perfect discrimination, while 0.5 indicates random performance.

\paragraph{AUC-PR} Area under the Precision-Recall curve, particularly informative for imbalanced datasets where positive class prevalence is low. AUC-PR emphasizes performance on the minority class (spam) more than AUC-ROC.

\subsection{Statistical Analysis: Detailed Formulations}
\label{appendix:statistics}

\subsubsection{Confidence Interval Calculation}

For each experimental configuration, we compute 95\% confidence intervals using the t-distribution:
\begin{equation}
CI_{95\%} = \mu \pm t_{0.025, R-1} \cdot \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{R}}
\end{equation}
where $\mu$ is the sample mean across $R = 20$ groups, $\sigma$ is the sample standard deviation, and $t_{0.025, R-1}$ is the critical value from Student's t-distribution with 19 degrees of freedom. For large sample sizes, $t_{0.025, 19} \approx 1.96$.

\subsubsection{Effect Size: Cohen's d}

Cohen's $d$ standardized effect size quantifies the magnitude of performance differences in standard deviation units:
\begin{equation}
d = \frac{\mu_{\text{baseline}} - \mu_{\text{ratio}}}{\sigma_{\text{pooled}}}
\end{equation}
where the pooled standard deviation is:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\text{pooled}} = \sqrt{\frac{(R-1)\sigma_{\text{baseline}}^2 + (R-1)\sigma_{\text{ratio}}^2}{2R - 2}}
\end{equation}

Effect size interpretation follows Cohen's conventional guidelines:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Negligible: $|d| < 0.2$
    \item Small: $0.2 \leq |d| < 0.5$
    \item Medium: $0.5 \leq |d| < 0.8$
    \item Large: $|d| \geq 0.8$
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Sensitivity Regression Analysis}

Linear regression models performance as a function of synthetic proportion:
\begin{equation}
\text{Performance}_i = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \cdot \theta_i + \epsilon_i
\end{equation}
where $\theta_i \in \{0, 10, 20, ..., 100\}$ is the synthetic ratio (in percentage), $\beta_0$ is the intercept (estimated baseline performance), $\beta_1$ is the slope (sensitivity coefficient), and $\epsilon_i$ is the residual error.

The coefficient of determination $R^2$ quantifies goodness of fit:
\begin{equation}
R^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum_i (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}{\sum_i (y_i - \bar{y})^2}
\end{equation}
where $y_i$ is the observed performance, $\hat{y}_i$ is the predicted performance, and $\bar{y}$ is the mean performance. High $R^2$ ($> 0.9$) indicates strong linear relationship, while low $R^2$ ($< 0.7$) suggests non-linear patterns or threshold effects.

    \item Predictable behavior: No extreme outliers

\paragraph{Correlation Analysis:}

We observe a negative correlation between baseline performance and SMOTE improvement ($r = -0.47$, $p = 0.036$):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Low baseline groups} (F1 $< 0.75$): Mean improvement +9.8\% (Groups 2, 10, 11, 12, 14, 16, 19)

\end{itemize}

This correlation supports the distribution alignment hypothesis: groups with insufficient real data coverage (low baselines) benefit from SMOTE's interpolation, while groups with already-good coverage (high baselines) experience disruption from synthetic augmentation.
