\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Spam and phishing email detection represents a longstanding challenge in cybersecurity, with adversarial threats continuously evolving to evade detection systems. Early approaches relied on rule-based filtering and manually curated blacklists, which proved fragile against adaptive attackers. The field subsequently transitioned toward machine learning-based detection, with statistical classifiers including Naive Bayes, Support Vector Machines, and ensemble methods demonstrating effectiveness across diverse datasets ~\cite{alhuzali2025depth}. Recent advances in deep learning, particularly transformer-based models, have further improved detection capabilities ~\cite{altwaijry2024advancing}, with systematic reviews ~\cite{thakur2023systematic} documenting consistent progress across multiple approaches and datasets.


Despite these algorithmic advances, severe class imbalance remains a fundamental limitation for practical deployment. In operational settings, malicious emails typically constitute only 1-10\% of total traffic, with some environments experiencing even more extreme ratios. This imbalance causes machine learning models to develop strong bias toward majority classes, resulting in poor sensitivity to critical minority class detection~\cite{5356528}. Wang et al.~\cite{wang2025hybrid} demonstrated that deep learning models exhibit robustness in spam tweet detection by integrating content-based and user-based features. Lian et al.~\cite{lian2021robustness} demonstrated the importance of systematic evaluation under varying imbalance conditions, showing that model performance degrades unpredictably as imbalance severity increases. Recent work ~\cite{shanmugam2024addressing} has explored machine learning approaches under extreme imbalance ratios, while specialized research ~\cite{milosevic2022extreme} has addressed scenarios where minority classes constitute less than 1\% of the data. Traditional solutions including cost-sensitive learning and threshold adjustment provide some improvement but cannot fundamentally overcome the limitation of insufficient minority class training examples ~\cite{gao2025comprehensivesurveyimbalanceddata}. This scarcity of representative training data motivates the exploration of synthetic data generation.

The challenge of class imbalance has driven substantial research into synthetic data generation methods. Traditional approaches emerged from statistical and data mining communities, with the Synthetic Minority Over-sampling TEchnique (SMOTE)~\cite{chawla2002smote} establishing the foundational paradigm. Parallel developments in natural language processing have explored text-specific augmentation techniques including Easy Data Augmentation (EDA) and back-translation ~\cite{shorten2021text}, with comparative studies ~\cite{ghadekar2023text} assessing their effectiveness. While these techniques provide some diversity, they lack semantic understanding and cannot generate content with specific characteristics while maintaining realistic coherence across multiple attributes ~\cite{ghadekar2023text}.

The emergence of deep generative models marked a significant paradigm shift. Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) learn complex data distributions through adversarial training, enabling generation of high-fidelity synthetic samples. Ammara et al.~\cite{ammara2025syntheticnetworktrafficdata} demonstrated GAN-based methods including CTGAN and CopulaGAN outperforming traditional approaches in capturing complex distributions, while Zouhri and Idri~\cite{10765501} applied CTGAN to address multi-class imbalance in cybersecurity datasets. Recent advances ~\cite{zhao2024ctab, bourou2021review, ammara2025syntheticnetworktrafficdata, rahman2025leveraging} have achieved substantial improvements on intrusion detection datasets. Systematic reviews ~\cite{agrawal2024review} and comparative analyses ~\cite{ammara2024synthetic} have documented consistent superiority of GAN-based methods over traditional approaches. However, GANs face critical limitations for our research problem. GAN-based methods including CTGAN primarily operate on numerical and categorical features, making them unsuitable for generating natural language content found in emails \cite{ammara2024synthetic}. While GANs excel at structured tabular data, they struggle to generate semantically coherent text maintaining logical consistency across multiple attributes ~\cite{zhao2024ctab}. Additionally, GAN training suffers from stability issues and requires substantial training data, limiting applicability in low-resource scenarios. Most critically, existing GAN approaches cannot support fine-grained control over specific semantic characteristics of generated content, such as subtlety or overtness of malicious features\cite{ammara2024synthetic, agrawal2024review}.

The recent emergence of Large Language Models has fundamentally transformed synthetic data generation capabilities for textual content. Pre-trained on massive corpora, LLMs demonstrate remarkable ability to generate human-readable text across diverse domains. Razmyslovich et al.~\cite{razmyslovich2025eltex} introduced domain-driven synthetic text generation using LLMs for cybersecurity classification tasks. Recent systematic reviews ~\cite{zhang2025llms} have analyzed hundreds of works across diverse LLM applications, while comprehensive overviews ~\cite{hassanin2024comprehensive} have explored their potential in cyber defence. Practical applications have extended to structured data creation, with recent work ~\cite{almorjan2025large} applying fine-tuned LLMs to generate synthetic datasets incorporating domain knowledge. LLMs offer several critical advantages over traditional and GAN-based methods. First, LLMs generate semantically coherent content maintaining logical consistency across multiple attributes, which cannot be achieved through feature-space interpolation or tabular data generation. Second, LLMs support fine-grained control through prompt engineering, enabling generation of content with specific characteristics. Third, LLMs require minimal training data, making them applicable in low-resource scenarios. However, LLM-based generation introduces new challenges. Generated content quality is highly sensitive to prompt design, yet systematic evaluation of prompt engineering strategies specifically for cybersecurity data generation remains limited. Recent work has begun exploring prompt variations across leading LLMs, with comprehensive guides \cite{chen2025unleashing} synthesizing emerging practices, but these remain generic rather than tailored to cybersecurity requirements.

Despite substantial progress across traditional, GAN-based, and LLM-based synthetic data generation, several critical research gaps remain. No systematic comparison evaluates these fundamentally different generation paradigms under identical experimental conditions, particularly under the realistic severe class imbalance that characterizes operational cybersecurity deployments. Furthermore, while prompt engineering clearly affects LLM-generated content quality, systematic investigation of how different prompt strategies control semantic characteristics relevant to security applications remains limited. These gaps motivate our systematic evaluation framework for comparing synthetic data generation methods in realistic cybersecurity settings.
