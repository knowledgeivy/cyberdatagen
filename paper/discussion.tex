\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Our experiments reveal three fundamental patterns across all evaluation tracks. First, model-dependent performance asymmetry persists throughout: GPT synthetic data maintains superior training effectiveness while exhibiting higher detectability; Claude shows reduced utility and detectability. Second, ensemble architectures demonstrate consistent superiority over margin-based methods across all experimental conditions. Third, cross-model synthetic augmentation provides measurable detection improvements, with effect magnitudes varying by baseline performance, prompt strategy, and classifier architecture.

GPT-4.1-mini's superior generation quality stems from distributional alignment to real spam, preserving lexical patterns and statistical regularities that enable effective classifier training. However, this proximity creates a fundamental paradox: the same distributional fidelity that makes GPT synthetic spam valuable for training also renders it more detectable when classifiers are trained on real spam. Claude exhibits greater distributional divergence, simultaneously reducing both training utility and detectability. This suggests high-quality synthetic generation inherently requires balancing fidelity for augmentation against divergence for evasion—a tension GPT navigates through linguistic realism rather than distributional mimicry. The quality gap persists across prompt strategies and classifiers, indicating fundamental differences in generation capabilities rather than prompt engineering artifacts.

Random Forest's consistent superiority across all tracks stems from bootstrap aggregation's resistance to distributional shifts. Multiple decision trees trained on random feature and sample subsets capture diverse patterns while avoiding overfitting to synthetic artifacts. Voting mechanisms average out sensitivities to distributional anomalies, producing stable predictions across varying synthetic proportions and cross-model training scenarios. In contrast, SVM's margin-based learning optimizes single hyperplanes vulnerable to systematic biases in synthetic distributions, yielding linear degradation under same-model augmentation and non-monotonic behavior under cross-model training. Organizations should deploy Random Forest for stability despite higher computational costs, or SVM when predictable degradation patterns enable capacity planning.

Cross-model augmentation reveals that weak prompt training data provides larger detection improvements than strong prompts, demonstrating that diversity drives cross-model generalization more effectively than quality. This inverts optimization strategies: defenders should prioritize diverse, lower-quality data capturing broader feature spaces for detection purposes. Organizations must account for this bias, potentially requiring hybrid real-synthetic training to preserve recall rates in operational deployments.

LLMs fundamentally favor attackers through low cost, minimal technical barriers, and high effectiveness across both augmentation and evasion scenarios. Attackers can freely select optimal models and prompts for either augmentation or evasion objectives. Defenders face asymmetric constraints: effective augmentation requires cross-model data, ensemble architectures, and careful management of precision-recall trade-offs. Model quality gaps create advantages defenders cannot fully mitigate through architectural choices alone. Defenders' primary resilience lies in Random Forest's inherent cross-model robustness, which maintains stable performance across diverse generation sources. Overall, Generative AI fundamentally shifts the offense-defense balance toward attackers unless defenders proactively adopt ensemble methods and cross-model augmentation strategies.

Our evaluation acknowledges several limitations. TF-IDF captures lexical patterns but may miss semantic relationships where deep learning with embeddings exhibits different sensitivities to synthetic data. CEAS-08 represents 2008 spam characteristics; modern corpora may interact differently with LLM-generated content. Fixed class imbalance represents one operational point; severe or balanced scenarios may exhibit different degradation patterns. Our mid-tier models may not generalize to: (1) open-source LLMs offering lower costs and local deployment flexibility; (2) budget or frontier models with different quality-cost trade-offs; (3) specialized approaches including template-based generation, fine-tuned models, or hybrid generation methods. Our cross-model detection focus leaves same-model scenarios unexplored. Spam detection findings may not generalize to other cybersecurity domains requiring different feature representations or threat models.
